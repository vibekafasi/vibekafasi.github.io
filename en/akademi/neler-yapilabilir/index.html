Academy / Module 1: Getting started

What can you build with vibe coding, and what is hard?

Learn to sort ideas with a traffic-light method: what vibe coding handles easily, what needs care, and what needs an expert.

Lesson 4/56 min readUpdated: November 2026

After a few early wins with vibe coding, it's easy to feel you can build anything. Some jobs really are done in an afternoon; others take serious experience. In this lesson you'll learn to sort your ideas into three colors: green, yellow and red.

The furniture and building analogy

You don't need to be a carpenter to assemble a flat-pack bookshelf; you follow the instructions and it works. Knocking down a load-bearing wall in a building, on the other hand, takes an engineer who knows the job, because if you get it wrong, other people pay the price too. Vibe coding has bookshelf jobs and load-bearing-wall jobs too.

Green: you can start right away

  • A one-page landing site, personal page, menu or price list.
  • Calculators: profit calculator, discount calculator, shipping cost estimate.
  • Small helpers that run on your own computer: renaming files, combining spreadsheets, cleaning up a list.
  • Data conversion: turning a spreadsheet into another format, summarizing reports.
  • Simple games, quizzes and learning tools.

What these jobs have in common: only you or a few people use them, there's no one else's personal information or money involved, and if something goes wrong the damage is small.

Yellow: doable, but needs care

  • Forms that collect information from customers (name, phone, address).
  • Small bots other people use too, like a Telegram bot.
  • Tools that connect to another service: apps that use a marketplace's or an AI company's API.
  • Scheduled automations: systems that prepare a report or send an email every morning.
  • Sites published online that anyone can visit.

In yellow jobs the code still comes from AI, but the checking load grows. You'll learn topics like keeping secret keys safe, taking a backup, testing and setting a spending limit step by step in later modules of this course.

Red: don't go in alone

  • Building your own payment system or storing card details.
  • Systems where thousands of users log in, storing passwords and personal data.
  • Apps in fields with strict legal rules, like health, finance and law.
  • Agents that act automatically with real money or real orders.
  • Large systems that must serve many users at once, quickly and without downtime.

Red doesn't mean “impossible.” You can build a draft of these with vibe coding, but before opening it to real users, an experienced developer or security expert needs to review it. And for things like payments, the right path is to use trusted, ready-made payment services instead of writing your own system.

Watch out

AI can deliver a red job in working condition too. The problem isn't that it doesn't work; it's the security holes you can't see. “It works” doesn't mean “it's safe.”

Sort your idea in 3 minutes

  1. Write your app idea in one sentence.
  2. Answer the four questions in the table above, in order.
  3. Decide the color. If it's yellow or red, find the smallest, most harmless part of the idea.
  4. Make that small part your first project. For example, instead of an order system, first a page that only calculates the order total.
Tip

Big ideas start with a small, green piece. First build something small that works, then grow it.

Key takeaways

  • Start with green jobs: one user, no personal data, no money.
  • Personal data, money and other people's safety turn a job yellow or red.
  • For red jobs, build the draft, but have an expert check it before going live.

Test yourself

3 quick questions. Pick an answer and see the right one instantly.

1Which of these is a “green” project for a beginner?

Small tools that only you use, with no personal data or money, are low-risk.

2AI built a site with user login, and the site works. What does that mean?

Security holes are usually invisible. A system that works can still be open to attack.

3You want to build a site that takes payments. What's the right approach?

Payments are a red zone. Trusted, ready-made payment services take on most of that risk.
